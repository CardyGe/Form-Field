# Rebuild Kaleidoscope first, then earn the stage

**Neither effect is show-ready today, and they fail for opposite reasons.** Kaleidoscope is cheap but is not really a kaleidoscope: it is a 7-iteration line ornament that cannot take video, camera, another layer or feedback, and it runs at only 4.2 to 4.7 ms median at 4K on an M5 Pro. Fractals have legitimate maths but too little runtime discipline: Mandelbox takes 42.9 to 52.5 ms median at 4K even at half resolution, about 18 to 23 fps before compositing. The app also lacks the things a performer relies on, with no audio analysis, MIDI, OSC, tempo clock, flash limiter or GPU timing, and an output-window crash closes the picture instead of restoring it. **The first phase should build a standalone Kaleidoscope instrument on a seam-free symmetry core, with a proper measurement harness, because that is the largest visible gain at the lowest technical risk.** Show safety and recovery come second, Fractal performance third, and playability fourth. The defensible route to "industry-leading" is not raw GPU scale against Resolume, TouchDesigner or disguise; it is the most playable, trustworthy fractal and symmetry instrument, with structure-aware morphing, curated macros and phrase-aware timing. That claim is not earned until the acceptance tests below pass on real hardware. The six clips remain inspiration throughout: this plan extracts techniques for two separate instruments and proposes no recreation of any clip and no combined match-the-source composition.

**How to read the evidence.** **[M]** marks a measured or first-hand fact from this repo, its benchmark, or a local maths or capability check. **[S]** marks a finding from a linked external source. **[I]** marks inference, including every proposed target, effort estimate and priority. Claims about clip motion are limited because the clips were reviewed as 12-frame contact sheets.

## The audit: one effect is mislabelled, the other is unbounded

The most important finding is structural. **[M]** "Kaleidoscope" is one style inside a three-style fractal fragment shader (`src/rendering/fractal.ts:64-106`), alongside Julia Bloom and Mandelbox. Its symmetry is a single dihedral wedge of 3 to 12 sectors. It has three motifs (circle, ellipse petal and cross ribbon), no wallpaper groups, no log-polar or Droste mapping, no inversion, Möbius, hyperbolic or spherical tiling, and no over/under weaving. Its "zoom flight" is a two-copy crossfade over the last 22% of the loop, so it evaluates the shader twice during the fade. **[I]** That doubling is the likely reason its 4K p95 sits about 1.7x above median.

Julia is the strongest existing asset. **[M]** It has smooth escape, distance estimation, orbit traps, analytic stroke filtering and an exact looping dive through a repelling period-2 cycle. Its limits are 32-bit precision, a quadratic formula only, up to 256 iterations and one forced sample in live preview. Mandelbox is the performance problem: up to 100 live sphere-tracing steps, 12 to 14 fold iterations, a 4-tap normal, a 24-step soft shadow and 4-tap AO, all recalculated per pixel every frame at a fixed 0.6 step factor. It has no temporal reuse, over-relaxation, depth pre-pass or adaptive resolution, and its half-resolution image reaches the output through a plain copy. **[I]** At the High setting the worst case is 896 field evaluations, or 12,544 fold iterations, per pixel. That bound, more than the average, is what creates frame spikes.

Compositing, finish passes, mapping and UI all add cost, and lower-end machines were not measured. **[I]** With 22 samples, p95 is close to the second-slowest frame, so it is a coarse indicator rather than a stable tail measurement. Live readiness is the wider gap. **[M]** A fresh search of `src` and `electron` on 21 November found no `AnalyserNode`, AudioWorklet, Web MIDI, OSC, Ableton Link, Syphon, NDI, GPU timer query, parallel shader compile or photosensitive limiter. There are useful foundations too. **[M]** `src/live` already has camera capture (video only, with `audio: false`) and people, tracking and mask modules, and the mapping compositor already has a governor. Their suitability as kaleidoscope inputs or presence signals has not been assessed.

## Six clips teach six techniques, not six scenes to rebuild

The clips share a design lesson more valuable than any single look: **one clear structure at a time, strong graphic readability, colour travelling through that structure, and motion that continues through the symmetry.** The current effects tend to show ornament without that hierarchy. The table separates what was observed from what should be learned. Each technique belongs in one standalone instrument and should stand on its own, under its own name, with no preset attempting to reproduce a clip.

| Clip | Observed in sampled frames **[M]** | Technique worth taking **[I]** | Home | What not to do |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1, 15 s | Log-polar spiral of scale-like tiles; dark and bright frames alternate | Integer-lattice log-polar closure, filtered tile motif, perfect zoom loop | Kaleidoscope | Do not copy its palette, tile design or pulse |
| 2, 14 s | Heart and circle contours from fine spectral lines and dots; landscape and figure; looks pre-rendered or AI-generated | Contour line fields from distance estimates, spectral dispersion, shape morphing | Fractals | Not a real-time scene target |
| 3, 58 s | Thin neon curves in a mirrored lattice with swirl warps | Analytic line rendering, wallpaper folds, phase travelling along curves | Kaleidoscope | Do not make the line weight assume a laptop display |
| 4, 30 s | Over/under ribbons with tube shading and rosette symmetry | Parity-correct weaving, tube normals and contact shadow | Kaleidoscope | Do not copy weave height through mirrors |
| 5, 18.5 s | Hard blue/white rosettes, overlaps, posterisation, inversion, full-frame changes | Circle-overlap parity, stepped colour, designed section changes | Kaleidoscope | Do not use rapid full-frame inversion |
| 6, 30 s | Mirrored dot tunnel, colour cycling, trails, lens bulge, melt moments | Non-conformal tunnel map, filtered dots, energy-bounded feedback | Kaleidoscope | Do not allow feedback to accumulate into flashing |

**Clips 1 and 5 may contain strong flashing.** That judgement rests only on sampled frames: clip 1 alternates between dark and fully bright states, and clip 5 shows high-contrast full-frame changes. Neither clip has been measured frame by frame or passed through a flash analyser, so this report does not state that they are unsafe, or safe. Their pulse and inversion behaviour should not become a design requirement until tested.

## Kaleidoscope should become a source-agnostic symmetry instrument

The instrument should process any source through one signal path: **source transform, domain warp, symmetry fold or motif lookup, post-fold warp, colour or posterise, feedback mix.** Camera, video, image, another layer and its own feedback should all be first-class inputs. A small control set of group, spiral integers `(a,b)`, scale `λ`, phase and tiling `(p,q)` opens a very large visual range without exposing the maths as a wall of sliders. **[I]**

**Topology decides what can be shipped first.** There are exactly 17 wallpaper groups ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallpaper_group)), but they do not behave equally on arbitrary video. **[M]** In a local numerical test of 30,000 directed steps per group, only **pmm, p3m1, p4m and p6m** folded a non-periodic image with zero discontinuities, with maximum step change of 0.005. The other 13 groups jumped by 0.65 to 1.90. Dihedral rosettes were clean at n = 3, 6 and 12, while cyclic rosettes showed seams. The reason is that every edge of those four groups' fundamental domains is a mirror. Phase 1 should therefore ship D_n plus those four groups through direct folds, which need one texture tap per pixel. The other 13 should arrive later through a baked, periodised motif. **[M]** That overlap-add method reduced the largest step change across all 17 groups to 0.0031, but **[I]** it averages images, softening contrast, and its visual quality has not been judged.

**Filtering is the quality moat.** Users of a mainstream Kaleidoscope effect report [centre pixelation and patch around it](https://resolume.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=89090) with resizing and masks **[S, search summary]**. The fix is to differentiate the continuous pre-fold coordinate, carry those gradients through the accumulated fold Jacobian, and sample with `textureGrad`. This avoids the [`atan` branch-cut derivative spike](https://iquilezles.org/articles/tunnel) **[S, search excerpt]** and the wrong gradients produced by 2x2 pixel quads straddling a fold **[I]**. Procedural lines should use analytic coverage from the same footprint, then fade to average coverage as detail approaches the pixel limit. As pixels approach a log-polar centre, the motif should fade to its average colour once the footprint exceeds about one tile. **[I]**

**Log-polar should replace the crossfade zoom.** Map `w = log z`, multiply by `γ = b - i a ln(λ)/(2π)` with integer `(a,b)`, then tile. **[M]** A local check confirmed closure with a residual of 7e-15 and reproduced the published Print Gallery multiplier for `λ = 256` ([Conrad](https://kconrad.math.uconn.edu/blurbs/grouptheory/CstarqZ.pdf); [de Smit](https://pub.math.leidenuniv.nl/~smitbde/papers/bridges-2005-desmit.pdf)). The zoom must animate the tile-coordinate phase and wrap at 1. A plain real-valued zoom generally does not land on a lattice vector when `a ≠ 0`, so it will not loop. Offset-brick motifs also need an even row count around the closure. **[M/I]**

**Weaving needs a parity table, not a mirrored height map.** Kaplan shows that alternating over/under crossings are available on the relevant [even-degree line designs](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/publications/Papers/kaplan_2000.pdf), and CelticGraph proves a consistent alternating assignment for [every 4-regular plane graph](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02852) **[S]**. **[M]** A local crossing check found that a 90-degree rotation and a mirror bisecting a crossing both flip over/under, while a 180-degree rotation and a mirror along a strand preserve it. The determinant therefore cannot serve as parity. Each symmetry operation needs its own flip bit. Hyperbolic tilings are a strong later differentiator and are cheap enough to consider: **[M]** the fold averaged 1.42 to 4.38 circle inversions across six tested `(p,q)` pairs, with a maximum of 15, so a cap of 16 covers them. No GPU timing exists yet for any of these groups.

## Fractals should become a bounded, navigable family

The Fractal instrument should keep its escape-time and folded-space identity and stop carrying Kaleidoscope. Its first job is to make cost predictable. **[I]** Split `boxField` into distance-only and distance-plus-orbit versions, so normals, shadows and AO skip colour accumulation. Replace uniform 4x supersampling with one primary sample plus temporal accumulation or edge-only samples. Add a 1/4 or 1/8 resolution conservative cone pre-pass. Then enforce a per-frame budget by fading step, shadow and iteration counts. Over-relaxation deserves a test rather than a promise: [Enhanced Sphere Tracing](https://www.lgdv.tf.fau.de/publications/enhanced-sphere-tracing/) offers a safe over-relaxed method **[S]**, but [rotation can make Mandelbox distance estimates inaccurate and discontinuous](https://www.fractalforums.com/3d-fractal-generation/mandelbox-distance-estimation-problem) **[S, forum]**, so the gain must be measured per preset. The notes could not retrieve reliable step-reduction percentages, so none is claimed.

**Temporal reconstruction is the main route to 4K, but off-the-shelf upscalers do not solve it.** [FSR 1 is spatial and expects anti-aliased input](https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-superresolution/), so it will not cure shimmer **[S]**. [FSR 2 needs depth and motion vectors and targets HLSL](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fsr_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-temporal/) **[S]**. A custom accumulator should use Halton jitter, depth-aware reprojection, YCoCg [variance clipping](https://developer.download.nvidia.com/gameworks/events/GDC2016/msalvi_temporal_supersampling.pdf) **[S]**, luminance-weighted blending and tone mapping last. **[I]** Fold-parameter animation moves the surface itself, so camera reprojection alone will ghost. History weight must fall with parameter velocity and on cuts or strong hits.

**Julia needs less new maths than better presentation.** **[I]** Keep the existing looping dive as the default infinite zoom, because it avoids the precision ladder altogether. Add iteration and detail levels driven by pixel footprint, OKLCH palette LUTs ([Oklab](https://bottosson.github.io/posts/oklab/) has a lower hue error than CIELAB and HSV **[S]**), screen-space line widths from the distance estimate, and contour-field modes that make the structure more graphic. True perturbation zoom is real but should come late. [Only the reference orbit needs high precision](https://mathr.co.uk/blog/2021-05-14_deep_zoom_theory_and_practice.html) **[S]**, yet the reference worker, orbit upload, rebasing and glitch handling add substantial complexity for a capability that matters mainly in Mandelbrot-location zooms. **[I]** No frame-time benchmark for it on Apple Silicon was found.

**New families should follow runtime discipline, in this order.** Mandelbulb and pseudo-Kleinian/Mandelbox hybrids reuse the ray tracer and add the most range for the least architecture ([IMAGINARY](https://www.imaginary.org/node/2364) **[S]**). A fractal flame offers the highest novelty, because [log-density accumulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal_flame) **[S]** turns noise into a look, but it needs its own histogram pass and possibly WebGPU. **[I]** The aesthetic ranking is editorial judgement; no controlled comparison of fractal families was found. WebGPU availability in the app's real origin also remains unverified, because the local check ran on `about:blank`. Morphs should be direct only within a family. Cross-family changes should be designed compositing transitions, which keeps Julia, Mandelbox and Kaleidoscope as separate instruments.

## Live and installation readiness needs a written contract

"Performance-ready" should mean a set of pass/fail targets. The numbers below are **proposed engineering targets [I]**, informed by the sources cited, and none has been load-tested on this app. The local probe **[M]** confirms that this Electron 45.2.3 build on the M5 Pro exposes `TIME_ELAPSED_EXT` and `KHR_parallel_shader_compile`, but not GPU timestamps, program binaries or `drawingBufferToneMapping`. Shader warm-up must therefore happen at load, and extended-range HDR must not be promised.

| Area | Specification | Acceptance target |
|---|---|---|
| Frame budget | Derive thresholds from measured refresh: GPU p95 target 15.0 ms at 50 Hz, 12.5 ms at 60 Hz, 6.25 ms at 120 Hz | Under 0.1% missed presents in 10 minutes; no two consecutive misses in normal playback; p99 at or below 1.25x display interval |
| Governor | Per-pass GPU timers with CPU fallback; 30-frame p95; fast step-down, step-up only after 3 to 5 s | No visible oscillation in a 10-minute stress scene; final warp, blend and UI stay at native resolution |
| Scene change | Every reachable program linked and warmed before it can go live; sliders never trigger a link | Zero blocking compile or link calls after ready; longest stall under 1 display interval; passes from a cold cache |
| Output | 4K60 primary target; 8K canvas allowed with heavy effects rendered lower; RGBA16F internal, dithered final output | Passes ramp, zone plate, 1 and 2 px grids, radial star and timing bar on the real display |
| Multi-output | Separate windows for separate surfaces; seamless blends use one logical output into a splitter or processor | No frame-lock claim unless proven with a timing bar and capture |
| LED and projector | Moving bright lines at least 2 px, 3 px on low-resolution LED; full-contrast detail no finer than 4 px per cycle | Checked at nearest and farthest audience positions and through any show camera |
| Recovery | Source fallback under 1 frame; context restored under 3 s; renderer recovery under 8 s; GPU process recovery under 15 s; supervisor relaunch under 30 s | Each fault injected during soak and timed |
| Unattended run | Installation mode, guarded Escape, atomic state save every 5 to 10 s, watchdog, schedules, telemetry, safe fallback look | 72-hour pass before a show; 14-day pass before permanent installation; memory trend under 1 MB/hour after warm-up |

Electron provides the required hooks, including [`child-process-gone`, domain-block control and relaunch](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/app) and [display-sleep prevention](https://www.electronjs.org/docs/latest/api/power-save-blocker) **[S]**. Frame-locked multi-output is a different matter. Apple's [genlock path requires macOS 27.2 and specific M5 hardware](https://support.apple.com/en-us/148292) and describes camera sync with one background display **[S]**, while this machine runs 26.6.2 **[M]**. Treat it as experimental. Installation prerequisites such as FileVault, auto-login and power-loss restart must be tested on the deployed macOS configuration.

### The flash limiter is a risk control, not a compliance certificate

**The limiter must not be described as Harding-compliant, or as guaranteeing photosensitive safety, without independent testing.** No validated real-time WebGL limiter was found, and BT.1702, Ofcom guidance and WCAG were written for television or web content rather than live VJ walls, projection mapping or immersive rooms. Venue law and event-safety duties were not researched and need jurisdiction-specific review.

The default policy should follow the strictest practical reading of [ITU-R BT.1702-3](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1702-3-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf): no more than 3 flashes in any second over the area limit, 360 ms minimum between full-field leading edges, special handling for saturated red, and sustained-activity reduction after 5 seconds **[S for the standard, I for the product defaults]**. Rate-gate strobe, inversion, hard cuts and audio hits at the control layer, then measure the final image on a coarse tile grid and correct in the same frame by easing contrast toward local mean rather than cutting to black. Bypass must be explicit per show, visibly warned, logged and reset on launch. **[I]** At 60 Hz the 360 ms rule rounds up to 22 frames, or 366.7 ms, so quarter-note full-field hits must move to half-time above about **164 BPM**.

Two details matter especially for these effects. First, BT.1702's pattern guidance explicitly includes **radial stripes and dot rows**, with risk thresholds over 25% of the screen when moving, oscillating, flashing or reversing and 40% when stationary, while exempting smooth one-direction flow **[S]**. **[I]** Kaleidoscopes and tunnels are therefore a pattern-risk category even when they never flash, and a beat-reversing zoom can lose the protection a smooth one-way zoom enjoys. Second, measurement must happen on **display-referred luminance after tone mapping and before warp**. The research pipeline listed the limiter before tone mapping, but **[I]** a limiter measuring scene-linear values would be judging an image the audience never sees. Peak-nit calibration matters too: 20 cd/m² is only [about 1.3% of a 1,500-nit wall](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1702-3-202311-I!!PDF-E.pdf) **[I from S]**, and so low a threshold will need careful area gating to avoid constant false triggering.

## The opening is playable depth, not a bigger media server

The market splits three ways **[I from S]**. [Resolume](https://resolume.com/blog), [Synesthesia](https://synesthesia.live/) and MilkDrop-style tools are playable but shallow or scene-bound for this domain. [TouchDesigner](https://derivative.ca/download/pricing), Notch and Wire are deep but require the artist to build the instrument. Smode, disguise and Unreal are show infrastructure. **Synesthesia is the closest product benchmark** at [$99 Standard and $249 Pro during a promotion](https://synesthesia.live/pricing), and TouchDesigner with community toolkits such as [RayTK](https://t3kt.github.io/raytk) or [FOP](https://derivative.ca/community-post/asset/fop-3d4d-modular-fractal-operator-system/75417) is the closest depth benchmark **[S]**. Competitors are moving: Resolume's 2026 releases added [spectral analysis, depth estimation, segmentation and MCP servers](https://resolume.com/blog) **[S]**. Generic versions of those features will not differentiate this app for long.

The credible position is **the best instrument for playing fractal and symmetry space live**. Four capabilities carry that claim: structure-aware morphing without pops, eight semantic macros with safe ranges, a phrase engine with visible timing confidence and one-touch downbeat correction, and a reference-quality kaleidoscope with clean centres and seams. A built-in flash governor and an installation mode with novelty memory strengthen trust. These are **"not found in the sources reviewed," not proven absent**, and no competing product was tested hands-on. No cross-tool frame-rate ranking is claimed.

The research suggested zero-install browser delivery as a differentiator. **[I]** That does not fit this product's current form: `package.json` defines a private Electron app, and show reliability depends on Electron's process controls. Electron is an advantage here, because its main process can host local bridges for OSC and possibly Link, while Web MIDI remains available. The app should integrate with Resolume, TouchDesigner and mapping tools rather than attempt media-server scale, multi-machine frame-lock, a full node graph or general clip playback.

## One ranked roadmap, starting with Kaleidoscope

The order below follows one rule: **first make the instrument real, then make it safe, then make the expensive parts affordable, then make it playable, and only then broaden it.** Effort is in focused engineer-time and is an estimate **[I]**. The total is about **27 to 38 engineer-weeks**, plus elapsed soak time. Impact reflects expected value for live shows and installations. All targets are proposals to validate.

### Phase 1, the first phase: build the standalone Kaleidoscope (3.5 to 5 weeks)

**Phase 1 delivers a separate Kaleidoscope layer that can fold camera, video, image, another layer or its own feedback, using seam-free D_n, pmm, p3m1, p4m and p6m symmetry, analytic filtering and a true log-polar zoom, with a benchmark that can prove the result.** It includes three simple original generative motifs (line field, tile and circle parity) so the instrument works without an input. It excludes weaving, non-mirror groups, hyperbolic tilings, audio control, flashing behaviour and any clip-matched preset. Its strobing and inversion controls stay absent until the limiter exists in Phase 2.

Phase 1 addresses this by excluding strobe and inversion, but **any public show before Phase 2 needs manual flash caution and should avoid high-contrast full-field changes.** A fractal-first plan would attack the worst benchmark immediately, but Mandelbox gains depend on the harness and governor, and Kaleidoscope delivers a new capability where Mandelbox delivers mostly a faster existing one.

## What the evidence cannot yet support

The benchmark covers one high-end Apple machine and one render pass, and the 22-frame sample limits tail precision. Maths checks prove the symmetry and closure logic but are not GPU benchmarks, and the weave and most of the groups have not been rendered in this app. Clip conclusions rest on 12 sampled frames per clip, so timing, easing and flash rate remain unknown. Several technical pages (Quilez, Keinert, Syntopia, Segment Tracing, Bán-Valasek and the full Draves paper) could only be read through excerpts or abstracts, or were inaccessible, and Resolume forum evidence is search-summary only. The VJ pain points are directional and largely older forum material. Competitor absences reflect the sources reviewed, and no hands-on comparison was run. The acceptance targets are proposed by this report rather than industry standards.

## Conclusion

The audit changes the question from "how do we add more fractal features?" to "what makes a visual instrument trustworthy enough to perform?" Kaleidoscope has spare GPU budget and no instrument; Mandelbox has an instrument and no budget. Treating them as one shader has hidden both problems, so separating them is the architectural decision that unlocks everything else.

"Industry-leading" should be something the app can demonstrate rather than assert: a seam test, a morph test, a beat-holding test, a recovery test and an independently analysed flash test. If Form & Field can pass those on a real wall, it will hold a position the large platforms have not clearly claimed: **deep generative maths that an operator can play confidently, in the dark, with the music already running.**
